# Publisher 2021 Support Ends October 13, 2026: What Actually Happens to Your Files

October 13, 2026 is the end of support for Microsoft Publisher 2021 and Publisher LTSC. If you've seen the headlines and you're wondering whether your Publisher still opens tomorrow morning, here's the straight answer: **yes, it still opens** — but "still opens" and "safe to keep relying on" are very different things.

This isn't a scare piece. Here's exactly what changes, what doesn't, and what to do about your archive this week.

## The Two Dates That Matter

Two different things happened to Publisher in October 2026, and people mix them up:

1. **October 1, 2026 — Microsoft 365 Publisher stopped working.** If you had Publisher through a Microsoft 365 subscription, it's already gone. The app was removed from the suite and no longer opens. That happened; it's done.
2. **October 13, 2026 — End of support for Publisher 2021 and Publisher LTSC.** This is the standalone, one-time-purchase version. The app keeps opening. What ends is Microsoft's obligation to you: no more security updates, no more bug fixes, no more support calls.

So if you're on Publisher 2021, nothing breaks tomorrow. But nothing gets fixed, either — ever again.

## What "End of Support" Actually Means in Practice

For software you run every day, end of support creates three slow-burn problems:

**Security.** Publisher files open complex binary formats. After today, newly discovered vulnerabilities in Publisher 2021 will not be patched. Opening a PUB file from an email or download becomes a slightly riskier act each month. This is the real reason Microsoft (and most IT departments) tell you to move off unsupported software — not because it stops working, but because it stops being fixed.

**Bit rot by a thousand cuts.** Windows updates keep shipping. Publisher 2021 doesn't. Over time, printing quirks, font rendering issues, and add-in breakages accumulate with no fixes coming. The app won't die in a day; it will quietly become less trustworthy.

**The shrinking safety net.** Microsoft Learn documentation, support forums, and community knowledge fade as the product's user base migrates away. Six months from now, when Publisher 2021 does something strange on a new Windows update, you'll be on your own.

## The Migration Playbook: Batch + Verify

Most people migrating off Publisher have more than a handful of files — old newsletters, flyers, event programs, brochures. Doing them one at a time is how archives get abandoned halfway. Here's the playbook that actually finishes:

1. **Gather everything.** Search your drives for `.pub` files and put them in one folder. People consistently find twice as many as they expected.
2. **Batch-convert to PDF first.** PDF is the safe archival format — it looks the same everywhere and needs no special software to open. Batch conversion handles hundreds of files while you do something else.
3. **Verify every file — this is the step people skip.** Batch conversion has a nasty failure mode: the tool reports success but the output is empty, truncated, or missing images. In a batch of 200 files, five broken ones look exactly like 200 good ones in the folder listing. Use a converter that checks each output and flags the suspicious ones, so you get a per-file report instead of a false sense of completeness.
4. **Re-run the failures individually.** A file that failed in batch often converts fine with different settings, or can be opened in LibreOffice Draw and exported by hand. The verification report tells you exactly which files need attention instead of leaving you guessing.
5. **Convert the ones you still edit to an editable format.** For documents you'll keep updating, export to DOCX or rebuild in Word, Canva, or Affinity Publisher. PDF is for the archive; editable formats are for the future.

## What About Keeping Publisher 2021 Running?

Honestly? You can. For a while. If Publisher 2021 opens your files today, it will open them tomorrow. But weigh this: every month past end-of-support, the risk of *one* file not opening right — or one security issue — goes up while your alternatives stay the same price. The rational move is to keep the app installed as a backstop while your converted archive becomes the real source of truth. Bet on the archive, not the aging app.

## FAQ

**Does Publisher 2021 stop working on October 13, 2026?**
No. End of support is not a kill switch. The app keeps running. What stops is security updates, bug fixes, and Microsoft support.

**Is it safe to keep using Publisher 2021 after support ends?**
"Safe" is doing a lot of work in that sentence. For opening your own files offline, the practical risk is low in the short term. For opening files from email or the web, the risk grows over time since vulnerabilities will no longer be patched. Don't build your future workflow on it.

**What happened to Publisher in Microsoft 365?**
It was removed in October 2026. Microsoft 365 subscribers lost access entirely — the app no longer opens. Only the standalone Publisher 2021 and LTSC editions still run.

**What's the best format to convert PUB files to?**
PDF for reading and archiving — it's universal and stable. For documents you'll keep editing, convert to DOCX or rebuild in a modern layout tool. Do both: archive everything as PDF, and migrate the live documents you still change.

**How do I know a batch conversion didn't silently fail?**
You don't, unless the tool tells you. Always use a converter that verifies each output file — checks for empty pages, missing images, and truncated content — and gives you a per-file report. Silent failures are the number one way batch migrations lose documents without anyone noticing.
