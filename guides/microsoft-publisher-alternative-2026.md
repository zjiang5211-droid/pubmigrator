# Microsoft Publisher Is Gone: The Best Alternatives in 2026

Microsoft Publisher was retired in October 2026 — after 35 years, the desktop-publishing app for newsletters, flyers, and brochures is no more. Microsoft 365 subscribers lost access entirely; the standalone versions are unsupported. If Publisher was part of your workflow, you now have two problems: **opening your old files**, and **making new documents** going forward.

This guide covers both, honestly — including where each alternative falls short.

## The Alternatives, Honestly Ranked for Former Publisher Users

### 1. Canva — Best for Non-Designers Making Flyers and Social Graphics

Canva is the closest thing to "Publisher without the learning curve." Drag-and-drop, thousands of templates, runs in a browser. If your Publisher use was flyers, posters, invitations, and social media graphics, Canva will feel like an upgrade.

*Catches:* It's template-driven, so precise multi-page documents (like a 12-page newsletter with custom layouts) are awkward. The free tier is generous but some elements cost extra. And your designs live on Canva's servers — fine for most, worth knowing.

### 2. Affinity Publisher 2 — Best True Desktop-Publishing Replacement

If you used Publisher's *serious* features — master pages, multi-page documents, precise typography — Affinity Publisher 2 is the real successor. It's a professional layout tool (think InDesign at a fraction of the price), with a one-time purchase instead of a subscription.

*Catches:* It's professional software with a professional learning curve. Overkill if you made a monthly one-page bulletin. Also note: it can't open PUB files directly — convert those first.

### 3. Scribus — Best Free and Open-Source Option

Scribus is the free, open-source desktop publishing app. Surprisingly capable for the price (free), and it runs on Windows, Mac, and Linux.

*Catches:* The interface feels dated, the learning curve is real, and community support is thinner than commercial alternatives. Good for the technically comfortable on a zero budget.

### 4. Microsoft Word / Google Docs — Best for Simple Newsletters

Here's the contrarian take: most church bulletins, school newsletters, and club announcements don't need desktop-publishing software at all. A well-made Word or Google Docs template handles them fine, and everyone already knows how to use them.

*Catches:* You lose pixel-precise layout control. If your documents were simple, you won't miss it. If they weren't, you'll notice immediately.

### 5. Adobe InDesign — Best for Professionals (and Most Expensive)

The industry standard. If you were pushing Publisher to its absolute limits, InDesign is where you were headed anyway.

*Catches:* Subscription pricing, serious learning curve, and complete overkill for a PTA newsletter.

## What About LibreOffice?

LibreOffice Draw can open many PUB files directly, which makes it a handy *bridge* during your migration — open old files, export to PDF. As a long-term Publisher replacement it's mediocre: it wasn't designed as a layout tool. Use it for rescue, not for your future workflow.

## A Decision Framework (60 Seconds)

- **"I made simple flyers and bulletins"** → Canva, or just Word/Google Docs templates.
- **"I made multi-page newsletters with real layout work"** → Affinity Publisher 2.
- **"I have no budget and some patience"** → Scribus.
- **"I was basically doing professional design"** → InDesign.
- **"I just need my old files readable"** → You don't need an alternative at all. Convert everything to PDF and move on.

## FAQ

**Did Microsoft replace Publisher with something?**
No. Microsoft pointed users toward Word, PowerPoint, and Designer templates, but there's no direct Publisher successor in the Microsoft 365 lineup.

**Can any alternative open PUB files directly?**
LibreOffice can open many of them. Affinity, Canva, and InDesign cannot — convert your PUBs to PDF or DOCX first.

**Is it worth learning Affinity Publisher 2 for a monthly newsletter?**
Probably not — that's Canva-or-Word territory. Affinity earns its learning curve on complex, multi-page documents.

**What happens to my old PUB files if I do nothing?**
Nothing dramatic — they just sit there, unreadable, slowly becoming mysterious. Every year you wait, you remember less about what's in them. Convert now.

**I'm a teacher with 200 old newsletters. What's the minimal-effort path?**
Batch-convert everything to PDF (for the archive), pick the 5–10 templates you actually reuse and rebuild those in Canva or Word, and let the rest live happily as PDFs.
